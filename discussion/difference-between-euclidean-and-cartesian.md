# What is the difference between Euclidean and Cartesian?

## The Question

Euclidean and Cartesian are often mentioned together — what actually distinguishes them?

## Short Answer

They aren't opposites — they describe different things. **Euclidean geometry** is a system of geometric axioms and rules; **Cartesian coordinates** are a method for labeling points with numbers.

---

## Comparison

| | Euclidean | Cartesian |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A *system of geometry* (axioms, points, lines, angles) from Euclid's *Elements* | A *coordinate system* (x, y, z axes) devised by Descartes to represent geometric objects with numbers |
| Focus | Logical/axiomatic relationships between shapes | Numerical/algebraic representation of position |
| Era | ~300 BCE | ~1600s CE |
| Relation | Describes flat ("flat" = zero-curvature) space, as opposed to non-Euclidean (curved) geometries like spherical/hyperbolic | A *tool* that can express Euclidean geometry algebraically (analytic geometry) — but coordinates can also describe non-Euclidean spaces |

---

## Key Point

Cartesian coordinates are a *method for labeling points with numbers*; Euclidean geometry is a *set of rules about space* (parallel lines never meet, angles of a triangle sum to 180°, etc.). Cartesian coordinates can be used to do Euclidean geometry (that's what "analytic geometry" is), but coordinate-like systems can also describe non-Euclidean spaces — for example, curved spacetime in general relativity.
